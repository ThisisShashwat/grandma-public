Your hours come from Hackatime, a free tool made by Hack Club that tracks the time you spend coding. No Hackatime, no hours. No hours, no cookies.

## Do this

1. Follow the official guide: **[Hackatime docs](https://hackatime.hackclub.com/docs)**. It has a page for each editor.
2. Code for a few minutes in your editor.
3. Look at your Hackatime dashboard and check that the time showed up.

Do step 3 now. Grandma has seen too many people build for days and then find an empty dashboard.

::: tip
Hackatime is made and maintained by Hack Club, so its own docs are the place to look for setup and fixes. We link to them rather than copy them, because they change.
:::

## Connecting your account

While Grandma is getting to know you, she asks you to connect your Hackatime account. You press the button, Hackatime asks whether she may take a look, and you say yes. Then she can read your Hackatime numbers on her own, without sending you back to Hackatime every time.

What she reads: your Hackatime account number, the GitHub name Hackatime has for you (it appears on your public profile), the list of your projects, and the hours on the projects you tick. She keeps the key encrypted and never shows it to anybody.

## Counting your hours

When you hand a project in, the note has a Check my hours button. It opens a list of your Hackatime projects, and you tick the ones that belong to this project.

- **Only new projects are listed.** A project counts only if you started it after your talk with Grandma. If a Hackatime project had more than ten minutes of time on it before you kept your demands, it is not listed. A few stray minutes from before are forgiven.
- **Tick one to ten projects.** Grandma counts the hours of those only, and only the time since you kept your demands. Time from before the talk never counts, and a minute logged to two ticked projects counts once.
- **One Hackatime project backs one project.** If you already used a Hackatime project for another project of yours, Grandma says it is already counted. Otherwise she would count the same time twice.
- **Names with a comma or stray spaces can't be ticked.** Rename the project in Hackatime and it shows up.

## Disconnecting

You can cut Grandma off whenever you like. Open your Hackatime settings, go to **Privacy and Security**, find the list of authorized applications and press Revoke next to this site. Grandma then cannot read anything, and the next time she needs your hours she asks you to connect again.

::: warning
An honest word, because you should know. A connected app's key can in principle also be used to fetch your Hackatime API key, and that key can add time to your account. Hackatime does not say so on its consent screen. That is why Grandma keeps the key encrypted, never shows it and never uses it for anything but reading your hours. It is also a good reason to revoke it when the program ends and you no longer need her to see your hours.
:::

::: note
After Grandma has handed over a result, you see how much Hackatime tracked and how many hours the reviewer approved, and so does everyone who opens your project page; if the reviewer approves less, the note says why.
:::
